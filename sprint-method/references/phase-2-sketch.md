# Phase 2 — Sketch

**Goal:** Generate a range of detailed, individual solution ideas — not group brainstorm. Diverge before you converge.

---

## Exercise 1: Lightning Demos

Before sketching, gather inspiration. Each sprint team member (played by Claude) briefly presents one compelling example relevant to the problem — from competitors, adjacent industries, or unrelated fields with transferable ideas.

Format for each demo (3 minutes / ~3 bullet points):
- **Source:** What product, company, or example is this?
- **Big idea:** What's the one thing worth stealing?
- **Relevance:** Why does it apply to our challenge?

Run 3–5 Lightning Demos. Then ask the user: *"What examples inspire you — from products you use, companies you admire, or totally unrelated fields?"*

Capture the "big ideas worth stealing" as a list.

---

## Exercise 2: Individual Sketching (4 Steps)

Walk the user through each step sequentially. Adapt the physical process to a text/chat context.

### Step 1 — Notes
Ask the user to review the Phase 1 artifacts (goal, sprint questions, journey map, target). Then ask: *"What stands out? What's the most important thing to solve for?"*

### Step 2 — Ideas
Prompt for rough, unfiltered ideas: *"List 5–8 rough ideas, even half-baked ones. No judgment yet."*

Help expand or challenge each idea with 1-2 questions.

### Step 3 — Crazy 8s
Fold the best idea into 8 rapid variations. Prompt: *"Take your most promising idea and give me 8 variations — push it further, strip it back, flip it, combine it with something else. 1–2 sentences each."*

The goal is to break fixation on a single form and find unexpected angles.

### Step 4 — Solution Sketch
Ask the user to pick their strongest idea and develop it into a **3-panel Solution Sketch**:

- **Panel 1:** Entry point — how does the customer first encounter this?
- **Panel 2:** Core interaction — what's the key moment of value?
- **Panel 3:** Outcome — what does success look like for the customer?

Rules for a good Solution Sketch:
- Self-explanatory (no presenter needed to explain it)
- Uses real words, not placeholder text
- Focuses on the customer experience, not implementation details

Claude helps refine the sketch with clarifying questions.

---

## Exercise 3: Recruit Customers (Background Task)

Remind the user that by Phase 5, they'll need 5 target customers to interview. Prompt: *"Who are 3–5 real people who match your target customer? How might you reach them before Phase 5?"*

This doesn't need to be resolved now — just plant the seed.

---

## Phase 2 Artifacts

Summarize and confirm with the user before proceeding:

1. **Lightning demo insights** — list of "big ideas worth stealing"
2. **Crazy 8s variations** — 8 rapid variations on the core idea
3. **Solution Sketch** — 3-panel description of the chosen concept
4. **Recruitment plan** — names or channels for 5 test customers (even if draft)
