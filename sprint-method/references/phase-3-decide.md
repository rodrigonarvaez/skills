# Phase 3 — Decide

**Goal:** Choose the best solution and translate it into a storyboard ready for prototyping.

---

## Exercise 1: Sticky Decision (Morning)

A structured, bias-resistant process for evaluating sketches. Run these steps in order.

### Art Museum
Present all Solution Sketches from Phase 2 side by side (if there are multiple from prior sessions or Claude-generated alternatives). Give each sketch a neutral label (A, B, C).

### Heat Map
Claude reviews each sketch and calls out specific parts worth noting — not overall judgments, just observations: *"Sketch B's onboarding flow is unusually simple. Sketch A uses social proof in an interesting way."*

Ask the user to do the same: *"Which specific parts or moments across the sketches catch your attention?"*

### Speed Critique
For each sketch, Claude narrates a brief summary (1–2 minutes):
- What the sketch proposes
- What's strongest about it
- What's most uncertain or risky

Invite the user to add anything that was missed.

### Straw Poll
Ask the user: *"If you had to pick one sketch to prototype right now — not forever, just for this sprint — which would it be and why?"*

This is a soft vote, not final. It surfaces preferences before the Supervote.

### Supervote
The Decider (user) gets **3 votes** to allocate across sketches or specific elements of sketches. Whatever receives a Supervote gets prototyped.

Ask: *"You have 3 votes. You can put them all on one sketch, spread them across sketches, or place them on specific elements. What do you choose?"*

Document the outcome clearly.

---

## Exercise 2: Rumble (Optional)

If two competing ideas each received Supervotes, run a **Rumble**: prototype both versions and test them head-to-head in Phase 5.

Ask: *"Are the two ideas different enough to test separately, or can we blend the best elements into one?"*

If Rumble: treat them as Solution A and Solution B going forward.

---

## Exercise 3: Storyboard (Afternoon)

Turn the winning sketch into a **storyboard** — a 10–15 frame, comic-strip-style sequence of the full customer experience.

Walk through it together:

1. **Frame 1–2:** Entry point — how does the customer discover or arrive at the product? (e.g., an ad, a referral, a search result)
2. **Frames 3–10:** Core experience — step by step through the key interactions. Include real UI copy, labels, and actions.
3. **Frames 11–15:** Outcome — what does the customer walk away with? How do they feel?

Rules for a good storyboard:
- Each frame is a single moment (not a summary)
- Use real words — no "Lorem Ipsum" or "Click here"
- Specific enough that someone could build from it

Claude helps fill gaps, asks about unclear transitions, and flags any frames that seem to skip important steps.

---

## Phase 3 Artifacts

Summarize and confirm with the user before proceeding:

1. **Chosen solution** — sketch label and brief description (or "Rumble: A vs. B")
2. **Supervote summary** — where votes landed and why
3. **Storyboard** — 10–15 labeled frames describing the full customer experience
